An agent opened the pull request.
An AI scanner flagged it.
An autofix agent patched it.
The pipeline went green.

Everyone on that PR was very productive.
Nobody on it was a person.

"A human approves before merge" is the usual answer. That's a button.

A button becomes a control only when:
1. The person could realistically say no.
2. The person has the evidence to decide.
3. The decision is recorded: who, when, which version, why.

Which changes need a person? Not the big ones. The ones that:
→ can't be undone by a revert,
→ touch tests, policies, permissions or the pipeline,
→ skipped, changed or overrode a check.

And give that person evidence, not fifty green ticks: the spec, the checks, every time the AI reviewer and a test disagreed.

In my harness the default answer is no. Nothing ships unless someone approves.
