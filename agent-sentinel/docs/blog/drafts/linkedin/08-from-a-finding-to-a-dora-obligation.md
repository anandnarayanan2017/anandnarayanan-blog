"Is it DORA compliant?"

I'm asked this about most platforms I design. A tool is not compliant. A financial entity is, and it shows that with processes, people and evidence. A tool can supply some of the evidence.

So I mapped Agent Sentinel to DORA and the EU AI Act one obligation at a time, with three columns: what it produces, the test that proves it, and what is still the firm's job.

Some rows hold up. Detection of anomalous agent activity. Protected, role-separated access to the record. Counting the telemetry that failed to arrive.

Some say "partly". A human reviews a serious finding, but after the action, so it supports oversight without being it.

One says "nothing". A finding's severity is not incident classification. It knows nothing about how many clients were affected. That is the easiest claim to overstate with a tool like this.

A mapping with a tick in every cell is less believable than one with gaps. An assessor who finds one overstated claim stops trusting the rest.

I'm an architect, not a lawyer, and the article says so.
